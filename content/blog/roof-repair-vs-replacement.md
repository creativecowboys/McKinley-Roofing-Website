Almost every roof we look at comes down to one question from the homeowner: can you fix it, or does it need to come off?

It's the right question, and there isn't a one-size answer. A young roof with one cracked pipe boot and a tired roof shedding granules on every slope can both start with the exact same ceiling stain. We would rather tell you a repair will hold than sell you a roof you don't need. Here's how we think it through, so you can judge any recommendation you get, from us or anyone else.

A lot of homes went up along the I-20 corridor in that stretch, and the ones built during that decade are now somewhere around 16 to 26 years old. That's right about when homeowners start asking whether the original roof has another round of repairs in it.

## Start With Three Questions

Before anybody talks about repair or replacement, three answers set the direction:

1. **How old is the roof?** Age alone doesn't decide it, but it changes the math.
2. **Is the damage in one spot, or everywhere?** A problem you can point to is a repair. A problem on every slope usually isn't.
3. **What's the decking like?** Shingles can be replaced a few at a time. A soft or sagging deck can't be fixed from the top.

## When a Repair Makes Sense

A repair is usually the right call when:

- **The problem is in one place.** A cracked pipe boot, loose flashing at a chimney or wall, or a handful of shingles blown off one slope.
- **The roof still has life left.** The shingles lie flat, still hold their granules, and aren't cracking or curling.
- **The decking is solid.** No soft spots, no sagging between rafters.
- **The leak has a clear cause.** A lot of leaks trace back to flashing and the spots where vents, chimneys and skylights come through the roof, not the shingles themselves.

## When Replacement Is the Better Call

Replacement starts to make more sense when:

- **The roof is past about 20 years old.** That's a rule of thumb, not a deadline, but Georgia's long, hot summers are hard on asphalt shingles.
- **Wear shows up on every slope.** Curling, cracking, bare patches and granules collecting in the gutters all over the house point to a roof at the end of its life.
- **It leaks in more than one place.** Several unrelated leaks usually mean the roof is failing as a system.
- **The decking is soft or sagging.** That has to be fixed from the deck up.
- **Repairs keep piling up.** If you're calling a roofer every year, the roof is telling you something.
- **Hail hit every slope.** Widespread hail bruising is different from a few wind-damaged shingles.
- **It already has two layers.** Code doesn't allow a third, so any major work means tearing both off.

## The Matching Problem

New shingles rarely match a weathered roof exactly. Even the same product in the same color looks different next to shingles that have baked in the Georgia sun for years, and manufacturers discontinue lines and colors over time.

On a back slope, a slight mismatch may never bother you. On the front of the house, facing the street, it might. If insurance is paying for storm damage, ask your insurer how your policy handles shingles that can't be matched before the repair gets scoped.

## Repair Now, Replace Later Can Be a Smart Plan

If your roof is aging but not failing, a solid repair can buy you time to plan a replacement on your schedule instead of in a hurry after the next storm. That gives you room to compare estimates, understand what drives the price of a replacement, and pick materials without pressure.

The key is an honest read on how much life the roof has left. A repair on a roof that's nearly done is money you'll spend twice.

## Questions to Ask Before You Decide

Whoever looks at your roof, these questions will tell you a lot about the recommendation you get:

- **What exactly is causing the leak, and can you show me?** A photo of the problem beats a description of it.
- **How much life does the rest of the roof have?** A repair only makes sense if the shingles around it will last too.
- **Can you match my shingles?** If not, where will the difference show?
- **Did you check the decking?** Soft spots change the answer.
- **If you're recommending a replacement, why won't a repair hold?** You should get a straight answer with photos, not a sales pitch.
- **What's covered in writing?** Ask what the workmanship warranty covers and for how long, and get it on paper before work starts.

A roofer who answers all six without getting defensive is usually one worth listening to.

## Where Insurance Fits In

If wind or hail caused the damage, your insurance claim may decide part of the scope. The adjuster determines what's covered, whether that's a repair of the damaged area or a full replacement when the damage is widespread. Normal wear and age aren't covered, no matter how the roof looks after a storm.

That's why the inspection comes first. Photos of exactly what's damaged, and where, make the repair-or-replace answer clearer for you and for the adjuster.
